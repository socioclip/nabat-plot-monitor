    How a mangrove plot gets scored

    Each plot is compared against the median of every other planted plot at the same number of months since planting, rather than against a fixed threshold. A plot planted three months ago should look like bare mud; a fixed cut-off would flag it and waste a site visit. Benchmarking against its own cohort separates plots that are behind from plots that are simply young.

    !Needs a field visitMore than 0.08 below the cohort median, once the plot is at least six months old. ~WatchShortfall over 0.03, or a six-observation trend falling faster than 0.03 index units per year. ✓On trackNeither of the above.

    The trend threshold sits above zero deliberately: a plot whose canopy has closed has a flat trend, and flagging on the sign alone would send a team out to look at noise. Trends are least-squares fits over the trailing six observations, with low-confidence observations dropped first.

    What this measurement cannot resolve

    • Tide state at acquisition. A plot imaged at high water reads low because standing water absorbs near-infrared. Uncorrected observations are drawn hollow and excluded from trends.
    • Mixed pixels in young plantings. At 10 m resolution a first-year plot is mostly substrate; the index cannot separate 40% from 70% survival in year one.
    • Saturation in mature canopy. Above about 0.6 the index stops discriminating between canopy densities.
    • Other photosynthetic cover. Algal mats and seagrass raise the index with no mangrove present.
    • The benchmark is relative. A site where every plot failed equally would show no flags at all.

    How a crop field gets scored

    Each field is scored weekly against expectations for its own crop and growth stage, not a single farm-wide number. The status is the worst of five checks:

    !Needs actionRoot-zone moisture below the crop's lower limit for two consecutive readings; canopy index more than 0.10 below the stage expectation; or a pest trap count at or above its action threshold. ~WatchCanopy index 0.05–0.10 below expectation; water delivered under 85% of crop demand for the week; more than 20 heat-stress hours in the week; or a trap count above half its threshold. ✓On trackNone of the above.

    Inputs and their limits

    • Canopy index — for open fields a satellite vegetation index; for greenhouses an in-house camera index. Palms read lower than fodder at full health because of the sparse canopy and bare soil between trees, which is why each crop has its own expectation curve.
    • Root-zone moisture — volumetric water content from one probe per field. A single probe can sit in a wet or dry spot of a drip line; treat one odd reading as a prompt to check the probe before changing irrigation.
    • Crop demand (ETc) — ET₀ from the weather station times a crop coefficient for the stage. Coefficients are textbook values and are not calibrated to this farm.
    • Pest traps — red palm weevil pheromone traps (palms), yellow sticky cards for whitefly (greenhouses), fall armyworm pheromone traps (fodder). Counts are a proxy for pressure, not infestation.
    • Yield outlook — a simple index of accumulated vigour against the season target; it is a direction, not a forecast.

    What would be needed before real decisions rest on this

    Ground-truthed survival counts and species-specific allometry for the mangroves; for the farm, calibrated probes, crop coefficients fitted to local water-balance data, and scouting records to validate trap thresholds. This is a triage layer — it tells a team where to walk. It is not a substitute for walking.
