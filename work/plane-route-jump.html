The problem

Why this needed to exist

The first question in planning any trip is simple: can I fly from here to there, and how painful is it? Flight search sites answer a different question. They want dates, passengers and a cabin class before they show anything, then bury the route behind hundreds of fares.

Before you care about price, you want the shape of the journey: is there a nonstop, which airlines fly it, and if not, where would you change planes and how long would it take?

The product

How it works

  1. Type a city or airport code. Suggestions appear as you type, ranked so major airports come first.
  2. A serverless function searches an open, weekly-updated dataset of every passenger airport and its airline routes.
  3. It finds the nonstop route if one exists, every one-stop connection, and, only when needed, two-stop journeys.
  4. Options are ranked by total trip time, assuming a 90-minute connection, and flagged when one airline flies every leg.
  5. A globe rotates to the route and animates a plane flying it. Pick any option to see it drawn.
  6. A one-line verdict sums it up: "Yes, nonstop with Emirates in about 14h", or "No nonstop, quickest via Tokyo".
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Answer first, details second

The verdict and four numbers sit above everything else. Most people stop reading there, and that is the point.

Open data over a paid API

Live schedule APIs need keys and cost money per call. An open dataset refreshed weekly answers "which airlines serve this route" for free, with no rate limits for visitors.

Prune before you rank

A busy hub has thousands of combinations. Detours more than about twice the direct distance are dropped, and two-stop search only runs when nonstop and one-stop options are thin.

Say what it is not

It shows route coverage, not today’s seats or prices. The page says so plainly, so nobody mistakes it for a booking engine.

Measuring success

What I'd measure

This is a prototype, so these are the metrics I'd use to judge it in production, not results I'm claiming.

Time to answerSeconds from landing on the page to reading a verdict
Route accuracyShare of nonstop results confirmed against live airline schedules
Option clicksHow often people explore a connection after reading the verdict
Hand-off rateSearches that continue to a booking site with the route pre-filled
