The problem

Why this needed to exist

In hotel distribution, one booking lives in three systems: the buyer’s booking, the hotel’s confirmation and the payment. They drift apart quietly. A booking call times out and the buyer retries, so the same stay is booked twice. A cancel call fails, so the hotel still holds the room and bills a no-show. A contract expires and the hotel prices next year on rack rates. Nobody notices until the guest arrives or the invoice does.

I saw this from the partner side for eight years in B2B hotel distribution. Reconciliation is usually a spreadsheet exercise done after the damage, and the patterns behind the errors rarely reach product.

The product

How it works

  1. Four SQL checks run across every booking, matching the buyer’s booking against the hotel’s confirmation and the payment: no hotel confirmation, price mismatch, refund owed and booked twice.
  2. Mismatches are sorted by how soon the guest arrives, with the money at risk beside each.
  3. For the chosen case, an Investigator agent reads the booking, hotel record, payment and API log, and explains why they disagree.
  4. A Resolver agent drafts one fix: a message to the hotel or a refund request. It cannot send anything.
  5. A person edits the draft if needed and approves or rejects it.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

SQL finds, AI explains

Whether two records disagree is a fact, so detection is plain SQL that anyone can read and audit. Language models only explain causes and draft messages.

Tolerance rules are acceptance criteria

A literal check flags every rounding difference and every booking the hotel hasn’t answered yet. A 1% tolerance, a 30-minute grace period and excluding group bookings keep the list to real problems.

Agents split by permission

The Investigator can only read data. The Resolver can only draft. Neither can send, cancel or refund. Splitting them makes each one testable and auditable on its own.

Measured against a planted answer key

The demo plants errors of known types and keeps the answer key outside the database, so accuracy is measured, not assumed. Two duplicates retried 25+ minutes later are missed on purpose: widening the window would flag real repeat bookings.

Measuring success

What I’d measure
