The problem

Why this needed to exist

A restoration team at a site like Jubail, Abu Dhabi, has dozens of plots and a few people. They can't visit every plot every week, so the question each morning is simple: where do we walk first?

Raw vegetation readings make that question harder, not easier. A two-year-old plot will always look "weaker" than a six-year-old one, so ranking by the raw number sends the team to the wrong places.

All values in the dashboard are simulated to demonstrate the interface and scoring method. Nothing is derived from real vegetation readings, sensors or farm records.

The product

How it works

  1. Each plot is plotted on real satellite imagery at its actual location.
  2. Its vegetation index is benchmarked against plots of the same age, not against the whole site.
  3. Plots that fall well below their age cohort are flagged and ranked into a "walk first" queue.
  4. A playback view shows how each plot has moved over time, and the full table exports to CSV for the field team.
  5. A second view applies the same logic to farm crops: canopy vigour, root-zone moisture, water delivered versus demand, heat stress and pest traps.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Benchmark against peers, not the average

The single most important decision. It turns a misleading number into a fair comparison and changes which plots get visited.

A queue, not a chart

Field teams don't need analytics, they need a to-do list. The output is a ranked list of plots to visit.

Be explicit about limits

The data is simulated, and the product says so on every screen, along with what the measurements can't tell you.

Measuring success

What I'd measure

This is a prototype, so these are the metrics I'd use to judge it in production, not results I'm claiming.

Hit rateShare of flagged plots that really need intervention when visited
Time to first visitHours from a plot falling behind to someone standing in it
CoveragePlots checked per person per week
SurvivalSeedling survival in flagged plots after intervention
