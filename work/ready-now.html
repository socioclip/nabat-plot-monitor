The problem

Why this needed to exist

On most delivery apps, an order that has left the kitchen can’t be cancelled. Customers whose plans change either pay for food they don’t want, or fight support for a refund. Either way the food is usually thrown away. The customer loses, the platform loses goodwill, and the meal is wasted.

A large Indian delivery app showed the idea works: it reported that customers were claiming about three in four eligible cancelled orders after it launched a similar feature.

The product

How it works

  1. The cancelling customer sees the deal up front: a small fee if someone nearby takes the meal, the full price if no one does, and the model’s chance of a rescue.
  2. Reach is measured in road time from the rider’s current position, not straight-line distance. Only customers within 20 minutes qualify.
  3. Safety and fairness rules filter the pool: sealed packs only, no chilled items, never the same household, a weekly claim cap, vegetarians only see vegetarian food.
  4. A logistic model scores each customer’s chance of claiming, with a per-factor explanation for every score.
  5. The engine tests every discount level and picks the one that earns the most, counting fees, the rider’s extra leg and the value of winning a new customer.
  6. The offer goes out in small waves, best matches first, and a 2,000-run simulation shows the likely rescue rate and time to claim.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Call it what it is

The offer is “ready food, nearby, sealed”, with one honest line on why it’s available. Hiding the reason would backfire the first time someone noticed.

Waves, not a broadcast

Small waves keep the offer personal and stop customers learning to wait for cancelled food instead of ordering.

Price with a model, not a fixed discount

A slight discount is enough at dinner peak; a quiet afternoon needs more. The optimiser decides per order.

Restaurant always paid

Kitchens get their normal payout either way, so the feature never becomes their problem.

Measuring success

What I’d measure

Rescue rateShare of eligible cancellations claimed within the quality window
Waste avoidedMeals and kilograms of food not thrown away
