The problem

Why this needed to exist

A guest at a destination hotel spends far more around the stay than on the room: dinners, the observation deck, the aquarium, the spa. Most of that is booked on third-party apps, by walking in, or not at all. The group owns the venues but not the itinerary.

Human concierges convert well but don’t scale, and they only meet guests who walk up to the desk.

The product

How it works

  1. The guest chats in plain language, before or during the stay.
  2. The agent already knows the booking: dates, party, children’s ages, loyalty tier, dietary needs, the occasion.
  3. It calls tools for live availability and prices across dining, attractions, spa, kids club and room upgrades. It never quotes from memory.
  4. It proposes a plan with times and a total, and books only after the guest says yes.
  5. Every booking lands in the trip plan, and the revenue it creates is tracked against the room rate.
  6. New preferences flow back to the guest profile for the next stay.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Confirm before anything paid

An agent that books on its own is a refund queue waiting to happen. One extra tap keeps the guest in control.

Tools, not knowledge

Prices and slots change hourly. The model only plans; inventory systems decide what is true.

One suggestion at a time

Cross-sell works when it fits the moment. A Kids Club slot next to an adults-only dinner is help, not an upsell.

Graceful fallback

If the model is slow or down, a guided flow on the same tools keeps the experience working.

Measuring success

What I’d measure

These are the metrics I’d use to judge it in production, not results I’m claiming.

Ancillary spend per stayDining, attraction and spa revenue per booked room, against a control group
ConversionShare of proposals the guest accepts
Concierge deflectionRoutine requests handled without staff, with satisfaction held flat or better
Profile captureStays with at least one new preference learned
