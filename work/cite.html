The problem

Why this needed to exist

AI writing is fast, but in serious work speed is not the bottleneck. Trust is. A single made-up figure in an investment memo or a board pack undoes every hour the tool saved, so people end up re-checking everything by hand and the productivity gain disappears.

The real job is not "write me an answer". It is "give me an answer I can defend, and show me exactly where it came from".

The product

How it works

  1. Paste your sources: filings, research notes, transcripts, policies. Each one is labelled (S1, S2, S3).
  2. Ask a question in plain language.
  3. The model answers only from those sources and tags every claim with the source it came from.
  4. Each claim can be expanded to show the verbatim quote behind it, so a reviewer checks it in seconds instead of re-reading the documents.
  5. Anything the sources cannot answer is listed explicitly, instead of being filled in with a guess.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Refuse rather than guess

Saying "your sources don't cover this" is a feature. It is the behaviour that makes the other answers believable.

Quotes, not just links

A citation that points at a 40-page PDF isn't verifiable in practice. A verbatim quote is.

Bring-your-own sources

No hidden web search. The user controls exactly what the model is allowed to know, which is what regulated teams need.

Measuring success

What I'd measure

This is a prototype, so these are the metrics I'd use to judge it in production, not results I'm claiming.

Citation precisionShare of claims whose quoted source actually supports them
Unsupported-claim rateClaims with no source. Target: zero
Time to verifyMinutes a reviewer spends checking an answer versus the manual baseline
Repeat useShare of users who come back for a second research task within a week
