The problem

Why this needed to exist

A developer sends the same Bill of Quantities to several contractors. Each returns it in its own spreadsheet: reworded lines, different units, a few items quietly left out, an arithmetic slip, a provisional sum trimmed, and qualifications buried in the notes. The lowest number on the cover page is rarely the lowest real price.

Quantity surveyors spend days levelling bids by hand before anyone can make an award decision, and the job repeats for every package on every tower.

The product

How it works

  1. Reads each bid as received and maps every line to the tender BOQ, expanding trade shorthand, comparing sizes and grades, and converting units such as kg to tonnes.
  2. Sends only low-confidence matches to the QS for a one-click check.
  3. Checks every bid for arithmetic errors, missing or unpriced items, changed quantities, altered provisional sums and risky qualifications, each with its money impact.
  4. Compares every rate with a should-cost range from past tenders and flags front-loading.
  5. Levels the bids with transparent, adjustable rules, so the ranking can be challenged and defended.
  6. Recommends an award, lists what to negotiate and the clarifications to send, and drafts the committee memo with AI from the computed figures.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Rules first, AI where it helps

Levelling has to stand up in an audit, so the arithmetic and adjustments are deterministic. Language models do the reading and the writing, not the maths.

Show the rule, let people change it

How missing items are priced moves the ranking. Making that a visible setting turns an argument into a decision.

Low confidence goes to a person

A wrong match silently mis-prices a bid. Anything uncertain is routed to the QS instead of guessed.

Risk is priced, not just flagged

A rate far below the realistic range is a future claim. Pricing that risk is what stops the cheapest bid on paper from winning by default.

Measuring success

What I’d measure

These are the metrics I’d use to judge it in production, not results I’m claiming.

Levelling timeHours from bids received to a levelled comparison, per package

Findings caughtErrors, exclusions and qualifications found before award versus after
