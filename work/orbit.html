The problem

Why this needed to exist

Satellites now photograph almost every port, airport and construction site on earth, again and again. The data is there. The bottleneck is that the people who need answers from it, a port operations lead, a project owner, a policy team, can’t write a query, read a spectral band or run a change-detection model.

So the real job is not “show me imagery”. It is “tell me what changed at this place, how sure you are, and what you can’t see”, in language a decision-maker can act on.

The product

How it works

  1. Pick a site (UAE ports, the Hormuz coast, megaprojects) or pan the map to frame any area. A dashed box shows exactly what will be analysed.
  2. Orbit finds the dates when the imagery for that frame actually changed, and labels each with its real capture date, provider and resolution.
  3. It stitches the same frame at two dates and shows them in a swipe viewer.
  4. Ask a question in plain English. A vision model compares both images and returns a structured analyst note: a direct answer, estimated counts as ranges, what changed and where, a confidence level, and what the imagery can’t tell you.
  5. Suggested follow-ups keep the analysis going without the user having to know what to ask.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Change is the signal

One image answers “what is there”. Two answer “what’s happening”, which is usually the real question. Two dates is the default, not an add-on.

A note, not a chat

Every answer has the same shape, so a reader can scan it in seconds and compare notes across sites. The conversation is the follow-up, not the format.

Confidence and limits are mandatory

Counts are ranges, sensor and season differences are not reported as change, and the model must say what the imagery can’t show. That is what makes the rest believable.

Honest about the data

Real capture dates and sources on every image, and only dates where the imagery genuinely changed. Archive imagery is labelled as archive, never presented as live.

Measuring success

What I’d measure

This is a prototype, so these are the metrics I’d use to judge it in production, not results I’m claiming.

Count accuracyShare of estimates whose range contains the analyst-verified count
False-change rateReported changes that are really lighting, season or sensor differences
Time to first readMinutes from question to a usable note, versus a manual analyst review
Escalation rateShare of notes that lead to ordering fresh imagery or a site visit
