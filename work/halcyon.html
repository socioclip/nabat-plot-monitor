The problem

Why this needed to exist

When one satellite carries payloads for several customers, every day becomes a negotiation. Each request needs the right payload, a pass over the target at a usable angle, and a ground contact afterwards to bring the data down before the customer’s deadline. Satellite time, antenna time and deadlines all compete.

Ops teams usually see this as a spreadsheet of passes. What a program lead needs is different: which commitments are safe, which are at risk, and what each fix would cost.

The product

How it works

  1. Orbits for every satellite are propagated with SGP4 every 10 seconds across a 24-hour plan window.
  2. For each request, the planner finds every pass where the satellite rises above the request’s minimum elevation over the target.
  3. It finds every ground-station contact above 10°, with one antenna per station and one downlink per satellite at a time.
  4. Requests are booked by priority, then deadline. Each takes the capture and downlink pair that delivers data soonest without clashing with earlier bookings.
  5. For each request that misses, it tests fixes: a later deadline, a lower elevation, another station, or a priority bump, and names any request the bump would displace.
  6. A copyable ops brief summarises the plan, decisions needed and station load. A gallery shows each simulated product as its downlink completes.
Product decisions

The calls that mattered

Show the tradeoff, not just the failure

"Not scheduled" is useless on its own. Every miss comes with options and their cost, because that is the decision a program lead actually has to make.

Greedy and explainable over optimal

A priority-first greedy planner is easy to reason about in a review meeting. An optimiser can come later, once people trust the inputs.

Outages as a lever

Turning a ground station off replans everything instantly, so "what if Svalbard goes down?" takes seconds to answer.

Honest about simulation

The demo constellation and products are clearly labelled as simulated, with links to real Sentinel-2 and NASA imagery of each site.

Measuring success

What I'd measure

This is a prototype, so these are the metrics I'd use to judge it in production, not results I'm claiming.

On-time deliveryShare of requests delivered before the customer deadline, split by priority
Capture to deliveryMedian time from capture to data in the customer’s hands
Contact utilisationShare of available ground contacts used for downlinks
Time to replanMinutes from an outage or new request to an approved plan
