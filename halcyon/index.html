How the planner works
  • Orbits are propagated with SGP4 (satellite.js) every 10 seconds across a 24-hour plan window.
  • A capture opportunity exists when the satellite rises above the request's minimum elevation over the target. Higher minimums mean closer to nadir and sharper imagery, but fewer chances.
  • A ground contact counts when a satellite is at least 10° above a station's horizon. Each station has one antenna, and each satellite downlinks to one station at a time.
  • Requests are planned greedily: priority first, then earliest deadline. Each one takes the capture and downlink pair that delivers data soonest without clashing with work already booked.
  • For any request that misses, the planner tests fixes (later deadline, lower elevation, another station, higher priority) and reports which ones would work and what they would cost.
  • The demo constellation uses synthetic orbital elements generated when the page loads. On the deployed site you can switch to live public TLEs from CelesTrak.

Delivered products

Products appear as each downlink completes. Optical frames are simulated renders of what the customer would receive. Use the links under each card to see real satellite imagery of the same site.
