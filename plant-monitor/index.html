      How it works

      Turn satellite and sensor readings into a short to-do list.

      Field teams have more plots than people. This tool answers one question each morning: where should we go first?

      1MeasureSatellites measure how green each plot is. On the farm, soil probes, irrigation meters, a weather station and pest traps add weekly readings.
      2Compare fairlyEach plot is compared with what it should look like: other plots of the same age, or the expected range for that crop at that growth stage.
      3RankPlots that fall clearly behind go to the top of the list with a plain reason and a suggested next step.

      What the colours mean

      Visit nowMangrove plot at least 6 months old whose greenness is more than 0.08 below same-age plots. On the farm: soil too dry two readings in a row, plants well below expected health, or pest traps over the action level. Keep an eye onSlightly behind (more than 0.03 below), or greenness falling over the last six readings. On the farm: irrigation under 85% of what the crop needs, many heat-stress hours, or pest counts climbing. On trackGrowing in line with what’s expected. No action needed. Natural referenceUntouched mangrove, used as the benchmark for what a healthy, mature canopy looks like.

      Glossary

      GreennessA satellite vegetation index (like NDVI) from 0 to about 0.6 here. Around 0.05 is bare mud; 0.5+ is dense, healthy canopy.
      Same-age comparisonThe median greenness of other planted plots at the same number of months since planting. This is the fair benchmark.
      TrendWhether greenness is rising or falling, from a straight-line fit over the last six clear readings.
      Unclear readingA satellite image taken at high tide. Water hides the plants and makes the plot look less green, so these are ignored.
      Soil moistureHow much water is in the root zone (% by volume), from one probe per field.
      Irrigation vs needWater delivered this week as a % of what the crop is estimated to use, given the weather (ETc).
      Heat-stress hoursHours this week above the temperature where the crop starts to suffer.
      Pest countInsects caught per trap or sticky card. Each crop has an action level set by its pest plan.

      Limits worth knowing

      • Young plots are hard to read. At 10 m satellite resolution a first-year plot is mostly mud; it can’t tell 40% survival from 70%.
      • Dense canopy saturates. Above about 0.6 the index stops telling dense from very dense.
      • Other green things count. Algae and seagrass raise greenness with no mangroves present.
      • It’s relative. If every plot failed equally, none would be flagged. The natural reference stands help catch this.
      • One probe per field. A single odd soil reading may be the probe, not the field. Check it before changing irrigation.
      • Crop water needs use textbook coefficients, not values calibrated to this farm. The yield outlook is a direction, not a forecast.

      Before real decisions rest on this

      Ground-truthed survival counts for the mangroves; calibrated probes, locally fitted crop coefficients and scouting records for the farm. This is a triage layer: it tells a team where to walk. It is not a substitute for walking.
