Demo · fictional hotel distributor, hotels and buyers

Every booking has three records. Find the ones that don’t agree before the guest arrives.

Halden Connect sells hotel rooms from six chains to eight travel buyers. For every booking there is the buyer’s record, the hotel’s confirmation and the payment. When they disagree, a guest arrives to no room, a hotel bills a no-show, or money sits unrefunded. Follow one morning’s reconciliation from the data model in step 1 to what product should fix in step 6.

1
Data · the model

Three records per booking, one data model.

Bookings, hotel confirmations and payments share one key: the booking reference. An API log and the hotel contracts sit beside them so agents can explain why records disagree, not just that they do.

Each table states its grain, what one row means. Get the grain wrong and a two-room group booking looks like a duplicate.

2
Matcher agent · SQL checks

Seven checks run across every booking.

The first draft of each check is literal: any difference is a problem. Switch to the tuned version to see what acceptance criteria do: a 1% rounding tolerance, a 30-minute grace period for bookings still in flight, and group bookings excluded from duplicates.

The demo planted errors of known types. The answer key is kept outside the database, so the checks can’t peek at it.
