Closed Loop · a customer experience demo for a convenience network (Meridian Stations is fictional)

Listen to customers, fix what bothers them, update and improve the experience.

Follow customer complaints all the way through: from the comment someone leaves, to the fix, to the reply they get back. Seven simple steps, top to bottom.

Customer  what customers say and see AI engine  what runs behind the scenes Experience team  decisions, owners and delivery

The scenario

18 stations across Abu Dhabi, 8 weeks of feedback, then a 6-week pilot. Change these to see every step adapt.
1
Customer · listen

Customers tell you, in their own words, wherever they are.

A two-tap survey from the receipt QR code, app ratings, public reviews, WhatsApp and the call centre all land in one stream, in English and Arabic.

AI engine · understand

Sort every comment into themes, and rank them by impact.

Each comment gets a theme and a sentiment. Impact weighs volume by severity, so a few safety complaints can outrank many about coffee.

3
AI engine · locate

Find where it hurts most.

Complaints are counted per 1,000 transactions, so a busy highway site isn’t flagged just for being busy.

Click a station to read what its customers said.

4
AI engine · find the cause

Line complaints up against what the operation records.

Feedback is joined with operational data: staffing, cleaning logs, terminal uptime, maintenance tickets. Where they move together, there’s a likely cause.

A pattern is a lead, not proof. That’s why step 6 exists.

5
Experience team · act

Agree one fix, one owner, and how success will be judged, before it starts.

The ten worst stations are split at random: half get the fix now, half carry on as normal for comparison, and get it later if it works.

The metric and the decision rule are written down up front, so the result can’t be bent to fit afterwards.

6
AI engine · measure honestly

Did the fix work, or did something else change?

A simple before-and-after at the pilot stations picks up everything else that changed too: weather, seasons, campaigns. Comparing against the stations that didn’t get the fix removes what both groups shared.

Try the “something else changed” options in the scenario bar.

7
Customer · close the loop

Go back to the people who spoke up.

  • Everyone who complained about this at a pilot station gets a personal reply.
  • The reply only claims what step 6 proved.
  • Customers learn that feedback leads somewhere, so they keep giving it.

Experience team · beyond one fix

Rules that keep it trustworthy, and what it’s worth across a network

Blame the process, not peopleStation and shift level only. Comments naming staff are masked before anyone reads them.
Personal data stays outPhone numbers, plates and card digits are stripped on arrival.
Arabic is first-classGulf dialect and mixed English–Arabic comments are classified natively, not translated first.
Decide the rule before the resultMetric, pilot length and decision rule are fixed in step 5 and can’t be changed mid-pilot.
Say “we don’t know yet”When two changes overlap, the result is reported as not separable rather than credited to either.

Editable assumptions, to be replaced with real data. The complaint rate comes from the pilot stations’ baseline in step 6, and the reduction is pre-filled from the honest estimate there. Most unhappy customers never complain, which is what “bad experiences per complaint” allows for.
