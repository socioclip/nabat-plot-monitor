Concept brief

Helping shoppers choose with confidence, in their language

Electronics are high-consideration purchases. Many shoppers know what they need the product for, but not which specification gives them that. This prototype tests one idea: a short conversation that turns a need into two or three well-explained choices.

The problem

Spec strings are written for experts

A typical listing title looks like this:

Lenovo ThinkPad E14 Gen 7 (Intel) (2026) Copilot+ PC - 2nd Series / Intel Core Ultra 5-228V / 14inch WUXGA / 512GB SSD / 32GB RAM / Shared Intel Arc 130V / Windows 11 Pro / English & Arabic Keyboard

Category pages already carry good buying guides and a laptop finder. The gap I see is a guide that works across categories, speaks Arabic as well as English, asks a clarifying question when it needs one, and is honest about the trade-off of each option.

Who it is for

  • Parents buying for children at school or university
  • Gift buyers
  • Arabic-first shoppers
  • Anyone comparing two similar models

How it behaves

  • Answers only from the product feed: never invents a model or a price
  • Always shows a cheaper option next to the best fit
  • Names one trade-off per product, such as an international version or an English-only keyboard
  • Replies in the shopper's language

Risks

What could go wrong, and the mitigation

Wrong price or stock

Read prices and stock from the live feed at answer time, and show when each price was last checked.

Weak Arabic answers

Build a test set of 200 real Arabic and Gulf-dialect questions, reviewed by native speakers before launch.

Cost per conversation

Use rules or a small model for simple questions; call a larger model only for comparisons and follow-ups.

Shopper privacy

Do not store personal details from the chat; keep only anonymous intent and outcome data for learning.

Rollout

A careful path to scale

  1. Phase 1Laptops only, on web, for 10% of traffic as an A/B test. High consideration and high basket value.
  2. Phase 2Add TVs and phones, Arabic launch, and the app.
  3. Phase 3Tablet mode for store staff, and hand-off to the store with "reserve and try in store".
Questions I would ask the team first
  1. Where do shoppers drop off most today: search, the category page or the product page?
  2. What share of sessions use Arabic, and how does conversion compare?
  3. What are the most common return reasons for laptops and TVs?
  4. How are online leads shared with store teams today?
